and the Adjusted Trial Balance

Basic Accounting | Grade 11

Strengthened Senior High School Curriculum

Learning Objectives

At the end of this session, you should be able to:

  1. Explain the purpose and need for adjusting entries.
  2. Identify the major types of adjusting entries (Deferrals, Accruals, and Estimates).
  3. Prepare adjusting journal entries for a business.
  4. Post adjustments and prepare an Adjusted Trial Balance.

Why Do We Need Adjusting Entries?

You already know how to prepare an Unadjusted Trial Balance. It proves that Debits = Credits. But is it perfectly accurate for financial reporting?

Wait a minute...

Have we recorded the supplies that were used up during the month?

What about the salaries employees earned in the last 3 days of the month, but haven't been paid yet?

The unadjusted trial balance often contains account balances that are not entirely up-to-date. Adjusting entries ensure that the revenue and expense accounts reflect the true financial position of the business.

The "Why": Accrual Accounting

Adjusting entries are required because of the Accrual Basis of Accounting. This is governed by two main principles:

1. Revenue Recognition Principle

Revenues should be recognized (recorded) in the accounting period in which they are earned, regardless of when cash is received.

2. Matching Principle (Expense Recognition)

Expenses should be recognized in the same period as the revenues they helped to generate, regardless of when cash is paid.

Categories of Adjusting Entries

Adjusting entries generally fall into three broad categories:

1. Deferrals (Prepayments) 2. Accruals 3. Estimates
Cash is paid or received before the expense or revenue is recognized. Cash is paid or received after the expense or revenue is recognized. Allocating the cost of an asset over its useful life, or anticipating losses.
  • Prepaid Expenses (e.g., Prepaid Rent)
  • Unearned Revenues
  • Accrued Expenses (e.g., Salaries Payable)
  • Accrued Revenues
  • Depreciation of Equipment
  • Allowance for Doubtful Accounts

Example 1: Prepaid Expenses (Deferral)

Scenario: On Dec 1, a business paid ₱12,000 for a 12-month insurance policy. The unadjusted trial balance on Dec 31 shows Prepaid Insurance at ₱12,000.

Adjustment needed: By Dec 31, one month of insurance has expired (been used up).

Calculation: ₱12,000 ÷ 12 months = ₱1,000 used in December.

DateAccount Titles and ExplanationDebit (₱)Credit (₱)
Dec 31 Insurance Expense 1,000
Prepaid Insurance 1,000
To record expired insurance for the month.

Effect: Increases expenses (lowering net income) and decreases the asset (Prepaid Insurance now has a balance of ₱11,000).

Example 2: Accrued Expenses (Accrual)

Scenario: Employees are paid ₱10,000 every Friday for a 5-day workweek (M-F). December 31 falls on a Wednesday. The employees have worked 3 days (Mon-Wed) but will not be paid until Friday, Jan 2.

Adjustment needed: The business owes employees for 3 days of work in December.

Calculation: (₱10,000 ÷ 5 days) × 3 days = ₱6,000 accrued salary.

DateAccount Titles and ExplanationDebit (₱)Credit (₱)
Dec 31 Salaries Expense 6,000
Salaries Payable 6,000
To record accrued salaries for Dec 29-31.

Effect: Increases expenses for December to follow the matching principle, and recognizes a liability (Payable).

Example 3: Depreciation (Estimate)

Scenario: The business owns equipment worth ₱60,000. It is estimated to depreciate by ₱1,000 every month.

Unlike supplies, we do not credit the Equipment account directly. Instead, we use a contra-asset account called Accumulated Depreciation.

DateAccount Titles and ExplanationDebit (₱)Credit (₱)
Dec 31 Depreciation Expense - Equipment 1,000
Accumulated Depreciation - Equip. 1,000
To record monthly depreciation of equipment.
Book Value of Equipment = Equipment Cost (₱60,000) - Accumulated Depreciation (₱1,000) = ₱59,000

The Adjusted Trial Balance

Once all adjusting entries are journalized and posted to the General Ledger, the accountant prepares an Adjusted Trial Balance.

Purpose

  • To prove the equality of the total debit balances and total credit balances in the ledger after all adjustments.
  • It is the primary basis for the preparation of the Financial Statements (Income Statement, Balance Sheet, etc.).

The Formula

Unadjusted Balance
+/- Adjustments
--------------------
= Adjusted Balance

Knowledge Check: Try It Yourself!

Data: The Unadjusted Trial Balance shows Office Supplies with a debit balance of ₱8,500. A physical count on December 31 reveals that only ₱2,500 of supplies remain on hand.

Questions:

  1. How much supplies were used?
  2. What is the Adjusting Journal Entry?
  3. What will be the balances of Office Supplies and Supplies Expense on the Adjusted Trial Balance?

1. Supplies Used: ₱8,500 (Beginning) - ₱2,500 (Remaining) = ₱6,000 Used.

2. Adjusting Entry:

DateAccount Titles and ExplanationDebit (₱)Credit (₱)
Dec 31Supplies Expense6,000
Office Supplies6,000

3. Adjusted Trial Balance Balances:

  • Office Supplies: ₱8,500 Debit - ₱6,000 Credit = ₱2,500 Debit (matches physical count!)
  • Supplies Expense: ₱0 + ₱6,000 Debit = ₱6,000 Debit

Summary & Next Steps

Key Takeaways

  • Adjusting Entries ensure the matching and revenue recognition principles are followed.
  • They always involve one Income Statement account (Revenue/Expense) and one Balance Sheet account (Asset/Liability).
  • They never involve the Cash account.
  • The Adjusted Trial Balance is the final checkpoint before creating Financial Statements.
